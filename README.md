## Using AI to Uncover the Secrets of TP53–DNA Interactions

## 1. 【The LEGO Assembly Line】

**Question:** What type of bond links amino acids together to form a protein? Which amino acids form hydrogen bonds with each other?

**Answer:**

![Protein structure](image/image1.jpeg)

Amino acids are linked together by peptide bonds to form a protein. Hydrogen bonds can form between amino acids with suitable polar groups, such as the backbone carbonyl oxygen (C=O) and amide hydrogen (N–H). These hydrogen bonds help stabilize the protein’s secondary structure, including alpha-helices and beta-sheets.

## 2. 【The Double-Track Highway of Life’s Blueprint】

**Question:** How do nucleotides assemble to form double-stranded DNA? What are the basic structural components of DNA? Is DNA right-handed or left-handed? List the names of the four DNA bases. Which bases form hydrogen bonds with each other?

**Answer:**

Nucleotides join together through phosphodiester bonds to form each DNA strand. Two strands run in opposite directions (antiparallel) and form a double helix. The basic structural components of DNA are a phosphate group, a deoxyribose sugar, and a nitrogenous base. DNA is usually right-handed in its common B-form. The four DNA bases are adenine (A), thymine (T), guanine (G), and cytosine (C). Adenine pairs with thymine through two hydrogen bonds, while guanine pairs with cytosine through three hydrogen bonds.

## 4. 【Return to the Crime Scene】

**Question:** Why can a mutation (change) in just one amino acid of a protein sometimes lead to a serious human disease? Give one specific example of such a disease.

**Answer:**

A mutation in one amino acid can change a protein’s shape, stability, or function, causing disease. For example, in sickle cell disease, a mutation replaces glutamic acid with valine at position 6 of the beta-globin protein, causing red blood cells to become sickle-shaped.

## 5. 【Return to the Crime Scene: Searching for Clues】

Using the TP53–DNA structure (**3KMD**), identify one TP53 amino acid mutation that can lead to uncontrolled cell division and contribute to the development of cancer.

Then examine this amino acid in **ChimeraX**. Based on its three-dimensional position, determine whether the mutated amino acid is located directly at the protein–DNA interface or somewhere else in the protein.

**Answer:**

This mutation affects DNA binding and can impair p53's ability to regulate cell division and prevent tumor development. Arg273 is located directly at the protein–DNA interface, where it helps p53 interact with DNA.
